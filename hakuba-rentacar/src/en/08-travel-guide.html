Local Travel Guide

Hakuba & Otari Local Travel Guide

Hakuba and Otari are among Japan's most stunning alpine destinations, attracting visitors from around the world for both winter sports and year-round adventures. Just a few hours from Tokyo, these villages offer the perfect balance of natural beauty, outdoor activities and authentic Japanese culture.

🕒 Last updated: August 2026

📌 Key facts about the Hakuba area

  • Hakuba and Otari sit at the foot of the Northern Japan Alps in north-west Nagano, a few hours from Tokyo.
  • Hakuba hosted events during the 1998 Nagano Winter Olympics and is a world-famous ski destination.
  • The main resorts are Happo-one, Iwatake, Hakuba Goryu, Hakuba 47, Tsugaike Kogen, Hakuba Norikura and Cortina.
  • In summer and autumn the area offers hiking, cycling, rafting, canyoning and autumn foliage.
  • The area is known for hot springs, traditional ryokan inns, handmade soba and sanzokuyaki.
  • Facilities are spread across a wide mountain valley with infrequent buses, so a rental car is the most flexible way to get around.
  • Day trips from Hakuba include Jigokudani Monkey Park, the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route, Kamikochi, Matsumoto Castle and the Sea of Japan.

Winter wonderland

Hakuba is world-famous for its ski resorts, having hosted events during the 1998 Winter Olympics. With reliable snowfall, well-maintained slopes and breathtaking mountain views, it is a paradise for skiers and snowboarders of all levels. Otari, a quieter neighbour, provides a more relaxed atmosphere with charming local ski fields and hidden hot springs.

Because the resorts are spread across the valley, having a car means you can pick your mountain each morning based on snow and conditions. Every Hakuba Rent a Car vehicle is 4WD with winter tyres fitted from December to March, and ski carriers are available.

Summer & autumn adventures

When the snow melts, the Japanese Alps transform into a hiker's paradise. Scenic trails lead to alpine lakes, lush forests and panoramic peaks. Cycling, river rafting and canyoning are also popular ways to explore the valleys during the warmer months. In autumn the mountains come alive with brilliant red and gold leaves, making it one of the best times to visit.

This is also when Hakuba Rent a Car runs its green season campaign: 40% off all vehicle classes from April to November. A kei car starts at ¥6,600 per 24 hours including tax. Golden Week, Obon and other peak periods are excluded.

Local culture & relaxation

Beyond the outdoors, Hakuba and Otari offer soothing hot springs, traditional ryokan inns and locally sourced cuisine. Visitors can enjoy everything from handmade soba noodles to hearty mountain dishes such as sanzokuyaki, a Nagano speciality of chicken marinated in garlic soy sauce and fried. Seasonal festivals and cultural events give travellers a chance to experience Japan's traditions up close.

Whether you are chasing powder in winter, hiking through alpine trails in summer, or soaking in an onsen after a long day, Hakuba and Otari promise an unforgettable journey in every season.

FAQ

Questions about visiting Hakuba

Do I need a car to explore Hakuba?

Hakuba and Otari are spread across a wide mountain valley and local buses are infrequent, so a rental car is by far the most flexible way to get around. It lets you reach ski resorts, hot springs, hiking trailheads and restaurants at your own pace, and makes day trips to Jigokudani Monkey Park or Matsumoto Castle practical.

Which ski resorts are in the Hakuba area?

Hakuba Village has Happo-one, Iwatake, Hakuba Goryu and Hakuba 47. Otari Village has Tsugaike Kogen, Hakuba Norikura Onsen and Cortina. Happo-one hosted events during the 1998 Nagano Winter Olympics and is the area's flagship resort. With a car you can choose your resort each day based on conditions.

What is there to do in Hakuba in summer?

Once the snow melts, Hakuba offers hiking in the Northern Japan Alps, cycling, river rafting and canyoning. Trails lead through alpine flowers and forests to panoramic peaks, and autumn brings brilliant red and gold foliage. Hakuba Rent a Car runs a green season campaign with 40% off all vehicle classes from April to November.

Can I visit the snow monkeys from Hakuba?

Yes. Jigokudani Monkey Park is within day-trip range of Hakuba by car, and is famous for wild Japanese macaques bathing in a hot spring. It is open all year, though winter — when the monkeys sit in the steaming water surrounded by snow — is the most popular time. Mountain roads can be snowy and icy in winter, so a 4WD vehicle is recommended.

What local food should I try?

Nagano is famous for handmade soba noodles, and there are many soba restaurants across Hakuba and Otari. Sanzokuyaki — chicken marinated in garlic soy sauce and fried — is another regional speciality. Drive out towards Itoigawa on the coast and you can also enjoy fresh seafood and sushi from the Sea of Japan.

Can I reach the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route or Kamikochi?

Both are within day-trip range of Hakuba. Note that the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route operates from April to November and Kamikochi from late April to mid November — both close in winter. Kamikochi also restricts private vehicles, so you park at Sawando or Hirayu and transfer to a shuttle bus.

Is it safe to drive in Hakuba in winter?

Roads in Hakuba and Otari are snowy and icy through winter. Every Hakuba Rent a Car vehicle is 4WD with studless winter tyres fitted from December to March, which gives stable traction, and tyre chains are also available. Mountain roads can still be narrow with limited visibility, so allow extra time and drive carefully.
